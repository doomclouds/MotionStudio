# Explain one array through three views

Use the composition when the audience needs to relate a field's spatial appearance, value frequency and one local sample. The image says where, the histogram says how many, and the sample note says which exact byte. These are different views of one array, not three numbers copied independently.

## Choose the lesson first

The ramp lesson makes a flat255 region visible as a tall final bin; a large count is not an error. The wave lesson has a broader distribution and shows a specific low-middle byte. Both are synthetic teaching patterns, not source debug output, measured sensor data, an actual rendering defect or a model of a natural process. Do not label a histogram as probability when its bars are raw counts normalized to the largest bin.

Keep the field primary. Arrange diagnostics beside a compact field or beneath a wide field; preserve space around notes, rather than imitating application toolbars. The two examples deliberately exercise these different hierarchies. Attach a sample marker at(x+.5,y+.5), and explain that it is a presentation overlay not part of the counted pixels.

## Color and type have jobs

Grayscale preserves byte ordering without extra category implications. The first warm accent separates distribution and location from a dark neutral page; the second cool accent connects the lower diagnostics to a warm-paper overview. These are choices for grouping and contrast, not a ban on green. Pick another hue when branding/category/contrast justify it. The histogram accepts any valid hex but its labels remain light, so do not choose a light panel background without a separately revised typography contract.

Use the exact verified font and readable final placement. The small axis labels are supplemental; headline, sample note and count caption carry the lesson. Actual glyph bounds are preflighted, yet fitting text is not automatically comfortably readable after film downscaling. Do not squeeze, crop or shrink failed notes silently.

## Relationship to existing assets

This is not another scalar-field foundation: the additional value is the owned quantized model and its linked diagnostics. Existing colored-field, halftone and contour tools have different contracts. They cannot pass their handles here; arbitrary images/RGB channel histograms are outside the API. Binning, helper methods and views are one recipe, not independently counted families.

## Future motion without false claims

Use an existing absolute-time/timeline system to choose explicit valid phase or sample coordinates, then rebuild all changed views from the same frame model. Do not tween the histogram independently of the image. Shape-valid endpoints do not prove intermediate-frame reading or performance. Snapshots/seek order should remain deterministic; validate reversed/repeated/random times and decoded outputs when a real film is made.

A future reveal can show field, location and distribution sequentially, with enough reading time. For reduced motion, hold a chosen valid snapshot and use restrained opacity/attention cues; do not invent new data. Caching is a bounded caller decision, not an unbounded internal cache. This candidate contains no playback loop, animation, audio or real-time performance evidence.
